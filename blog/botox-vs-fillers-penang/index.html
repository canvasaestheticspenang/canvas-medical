Botox vs Dermal Fillers:
What's the Difference?

They're the two most requested injectables in aesthetic medicine, and the two most confused. Botox and dermal fillers are not interchangeable — they solve different problems, using entirely different mechanisms.

The short answer

Botox is a muscle relaxant. Fillers are a volumising gel. Botox softens lines caused by repeated muscle movement — think forehead lines or crow's feet. Fillers restore or add volume to areas that have lost structure, like the cheeks, temples or jawline.

Neither is "better." They are simply built for different jobs, and many patients benefit from both at once, in different areas.

What is Botox?

Botox is a purified form of botulinum toxin type A. Injected in tiny, precise amounts into specific facial muscles, it temporarily blocks the nerve signals that cause those muscles to contract. With the muscle relaxed, the skin above it stops folding, and the associated line softens.

It's most effective on dynamic wrinkles — lines that appear only when you move your face, such as frown lines between the brows, forehead creases and crow's feet at the outer eyes. Results typically appear within 3 to 7 days and last 3 to 4 months.

What are Dermal Fillers?

Dermal fillers are injectable gels — most commonly hyaluronic acid — that physically add volume beneath the skin. Where Botox relaxes, fillers restore or reshape. At CANVAS, our physicians use fillers to:

  • Restore volume loss in the cheeks or temples
  • Soften static folds that persist even at rest, such as deep nasolabial lines
  • Define or balance the jawline and chin
  • Add subtle structure or hydration to the lips
  • Correct under-eye hollowness (tear trough)

Results are visible immediately and typically last 6 to 24 months depending on the product and area, though the tissue gradually metabolises the gel over time.

Can you combine them?

Very often, yes. A typical combination treats the upper face with Botox — softening forehead and frown lines — while using filler to restore volume in the cheeks or refine the jawline. Treated together, the face looks rested and balanced rather than "frozen" or "overfilled."

Your physician will assess your face as a whole before recommending a plan. The goal is always the most natural result, not the most product used.

How to decide

You may be better suited to Botox if you:

  • Notice lines that only appear when you frown, smile or raise your brows
  • Want to soften forehead lines or crow's feet
  • Are interested in a preventative treatment before lines become permanent
  • Want a jawline that appears more slender (masseter Botox)

You may be better suited to fillers if you:

  • Have noticeable volume loss in the cheeks, temples or under-eyes
  • Want to define or balance the jawline or chin
  • Have folds or hollows that are present even when your face is still
  • Are looking for a specific structural or shape change
