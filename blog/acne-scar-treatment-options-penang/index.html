Acne Scar Treatment in Penang:
Comparing Your Options

Pico laser, microneedling and chemical peels can all improve acne scarring, but none of them treats every scar type equally well. Here's how our physicians think through which approach suits which scar.

Not all acne scars are the same

Acne scarring falls into a few distinct categories, and treatment success depends heavily on matching the right tool to the right scar:

  • Ice-pick scars — narrow, deep, pitted scars that go straight down into the skin
  • Rolling scars — broad depressions with sloped edges, caused by fibrous bands pulling the skin down
  • Boxcar scars — wider depressions with sharper, defined edges
  • Post-inflammatory pigmentation — flat red or brown marks left behind after acne heals, technically not scars but often treated alongside them

Most patients have a mix. That's why a single-treatment approach rarely gives the best result.

Pico Laser

Pico laser delivers ultra-short pulses of energy that shatter pigment and stimulate collagen remodelling with minimal heat damage to surrounding tissue. For acne scars, fractionated pico laser handpieces create a grid of microscopic treatment zones that trigger new collagen production, gradually filling in shallow to moderate depressions.

Pico laser is also highly effective on the post-inflammatory pigmentation that often surrounds acne scars, making it a strong option when discolouration is part of the concern.

Microneedling

Microneedling uses fine needles to create controlled micro-injuries in the skin, triggering the body's own wound-healing response and new collagen production. It is particularly effective for rolling scars, where the goal is to break up fibrous bands and rebuild the tissue underneath.

Microneedling can be combined with radiofrequency energy or platelet-rich plasma (PRP) for enhanced collagen stimulation, and is generally well suited to patients with deeper skin tones, where laser treatments carry a higher risk of pigment changes.

Chemical Peels

Chemical peels use controlled exfoliation to remove damaged surface layers of skin, improving texture, tone and mild superficial scarring. They are less effective on deep, pitted scars but excel at improving overall skin texture, refining pore appearance and fading post-inflammatory pigmentation.

Peels are often used as a supporting treatment alongside pico laser or microneedling, rather than as a standalone solution for moderate to severe scarring.

Why combination treatment usually wins

Because most patients present with a mix of scar types, our physicians commonly build a programme that layers treatments over time — for example, microneedling sessions to remodel rolling scars, interspersed with pico laser sessions to address pigmentation, finished with maintenance peels to refine overall texture.

This staged approach costs more time than a single quick fix, but produces meaningfully better outcomes than relying on one modality alone.
