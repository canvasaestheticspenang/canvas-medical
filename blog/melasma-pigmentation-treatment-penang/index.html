Melasma & Pigmentation:
What Actually Works

Not every dark mark on your face is the same condition, and treating them as if they are is one of the most common reasons pigmentation treatment disappoints. Here's how our physicians tell them apart.

Pigmentation is not one condition

"Pigmentation" is an umbrella term. In our clinic, the three concerns that walk through the door most often are:

  • Melasma — symmetrical, patchy brown discolouration, often on the cheeks, forehead and upper lip, strongly linked to hormones, heat and UV exposure
  • Sun spots (solar lentigines) — well-defined, isolated brown spots from cumulative sun damage over years
  • Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) — flat marks left behind after acne, injury or inflammation

They can look similar at a glance, but they behave very differently under treatment — and melasma in particular can be made worse by the wrong approach.

Why melasma is the tricky one

Melasma is driven by overactive pigment-producing cells that are unusually sensitive to heat, UV light and hormonal changes. Aggressive laser or heat-based treatments can actually trigger inflammation and worsen melasma, sometimes significantly.

This is why melasma calls for a cautious, layered approach — typically low-fluence pico laser settings, tailored topical regimens, and non-negotiable daily sun protection — rather than the more aggressive settings that work well on sun spots or PIH.

What works for each type

Type Typical approach Key caution
MelasmaLow-fluence pico laser, topicals, strict sun protectionCan worsen with aggressive heat/laser
Sun spotsPico laser at treatment-strength settingsUsually responds well and predictably
Post-inflammatory (PIH)Pico laser, chemical peels, topicalsFades faster if underlying cause is controlled

Where medical facials and peels fit in

Alongside targeted laser treatment, our physicians often incorporate medical facials and chemical peels to support skin barrier health and gently accelerate pigment turnover. These are especially useful as maintenance between more active treatment sessions, and as a gentler starting point for sensitive or melasma-prone skin.

The one non-negotiable: sun protection

No pigmentation treatment, however advanced, outperforms consistent daily sun protection. UV exposure is the single biggest driver of pigment recurrence, and even a few minutes of unprotected midday sun can undo weeks of progress in melasma-prone skin.

Our physicians build a maintenance plan around this from day one, not as an afterthought at the end of treatment.

The CANVAS approach

At CANVAS Medical Clinic in George Town, Penang, every pigmentation consultation starts with correctly identifying what type of pigmentation you actually have, since that determines everything about the treatment plan. Our physicians calibrate laser settings to your skin and condition rather than using one setting for everyone.

We set realistic timelines, particularly for melasma, which is a manageable but often chronic condition rather than a problem solved in a single visit.
