Mounjaro vs Rybelsus:
Comparing GLP-1 Medications

Both are prescription medications used for medical weight management. Both work through the same GLP-1 hormone pathway. But an injection and a tablet behave very differently in the body. Here's what our physicians consider.

This article is for general education only and does not constitute medical advice. Mounjaro and Rybelsus are prescription-only medications. Suitability can only be determined through a personal medical consultation, including a review of your health history, at CANVAS Medical Clinic.

The short answer

Mounjaro is a weekly injection containing tirzepatide, which acts on two hormone receptors (GLP-1 and GIP). Rybelsus is a daily tablet containing oral semaglutide, which acts on the GLP-1 receptor alone. Both are used, under physician supervision, to support appetite regulation and weight management as part of a broader lifestyle programme.

Which one suits you depends on your health profile, your response to treatment and your preference between an injection and a tablet.

What is Mounjaro?

Mounjaro (tirzepatide) is a once-weekly injectable medication that works on two separate receptors involved in appetite and blood sugar regulation: GLP-1 and GIP. This dual mechanism is why tirzepatide-based treatment has shown strong results in clinical studies for weight management.

Treatment starts at a low dose, which your physician gradually increases over several weeks to help minimise side effects while your body adjusts.

What is Rybelsus?

Rybelsus (oral semaglutide) is a daily tablet, taken on an empty stomach with a small amount of water, that acts on the GLP-1 receptor to help regulate appetite and slow gastric emptying. It is the oral form of the same active ingredient found in some injectable semaglutide medications.

Because it is absorbed through the stomach, it needs specific timing rules to work effectively — typically taken first thing in the morning, at least 30 minutes before food, drink or other oral medications.

How to think about the choice

Mounjaro may be considered when:

  • You are comfortable with a self-administered weekly injection
  • Your physician assesses that dual-receptor action may suit your profile
  • You prefer not to manage a daily medication routine

Rybelsus may be considered when:

  • You prefer a tablet over an injection
  • You can reliably follow the empty-stomach morning dosing routine
  • Your physician determines GLP-1-only action is appropriate for you

Neither medication is appropriate for everyone. Both require a full medical history review, including current medications and any relevant personal or family health conditions, before a physician can prescribe either one.
