What is ELRAVIE Re20?

ELRAVIE Re20 is an ECM skin booster from Humedix in Korea, introduced in 2024. ECM stands for extracellular matrix — the scaffold that sits between your skin cells, made of collagens, elastin, proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid. It is the architecture that decides how firm, elastic and resilient skin feels, and it is what degrades as skin ages, thins and loses its bounce.

Re20 is a micronised human acellular dermal matrix, or hADM. Donated human dermal tissue is processed — Humedix uses a proprietary method it calls AlloClean — to remove cells, lipids and antigenic material while leaving the three-dimensional matrix intact. What is injected is the scaffold itself rather than a gel that mimics it, which is what separates this category from the skinboosters and biostimulators that came before it.

How it works

  1. Consultation and assessment. A physician examines your skin, takes a full history, and explains plainly that the product is derived from donated human tissue so your consent is properly informed.
  2. Mapping the areas. Treatment is planned to where the matrix has thinned — commonly the face, neck and décolletage — rather than applied uniformly.
  3. Micro-injection. The matrix is placed into the dermis with a fine needle or cannula, with topical anaesthetic where appropriate.
  4. Course and maintenance. Typically a short course spaced a few weeks apart, then periodic maintenance, with review so the plan follows your response.

ECM, collagen and skinboosters compared

These three get conflated constantly, so it is worth separating them. An ECM booster such as Re20 or Adité supplies matrix material directly. A collagen injectable such as Linerase, Collaju or Deusaderm places collagen specifically. A hyaluronic acid skinbooster such as Profhilo or Restylane Skinboosters mainly holds water, hydrating and plumping.

A fourth route, polynucleotides, signals the skin to repair itself rather than supplying material at all. None of these is simply better than the others — they do different jobs, and if what you actually want is contour, that is a dermal filler conversation instead.
