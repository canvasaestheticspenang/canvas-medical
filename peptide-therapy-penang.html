What is peptide therapy?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids — signalling molecules the body already uses to tell tissues what to do. Medicine has prescribed them for decades: insulin is a peptide, and so are the GLP-1 medicines now used in weight management. What the term "peptide therapy" usually means in a clinic setting is prescribing a specific peptide medicine, for a specific indication, after an assessment establishes that it is appropriate.

At CANVAS Medical Clinic in George Town, Penang, that assessment comes first and decides everything after it. Peptide therapy here is not a menu you choose from — it is a physician deciding whether a prescription is warranted, which one, and whether something more established would serve you better. Frequently the honest answer is that it would, and we will say so.

How a protocol works

  1. Full medical assessment. History, examination, current medications and supplements, and blood work where it is indicated — because a peptide prescription without a picture of your health is guesswork.
  2. An honest recommendation. Your physician sets out whether a peptide is appropriate, what it would realistically do, and where a better-established treatment should come first instead.
  3. Prescription and instruction. If treatment proceeds, a registered product is dispensed through pharmacy supply, with dosing, administration and storage explained properly.
  4. Review and reassessment. You are reviewed on a schedule to check response and tolerance. If it is not doing what it was prescribed to do, it stops.

Peptide therapy is not suitable for everyone and is not appropriate during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Some peptides marketed direct to consumers online are unregulated products of unverified content — we do not use them and we would strongly discourage self-sourcing. Any medicine prescribed here follows an assessment, and outcomes differ between individuals. Suitability is always confirmed at consultation.

Peptides and weight management

The overlap confuses a lot of patients, so it is worth stating plainly: the GLP-1 medicines used in weight management are peptides. Mounjaro (tirzepatide), Wegovy (semaglutide) and Rybelsus (oral semaglutide) are peptide medicines with large clinical trial programmes behind them.

If weight is what you actually want to change, that evidence base is the sensible starting point, delivered inside structured obesity management rather than on its own. Our physicians would rather put you on the better-studied path than sell you a more novel one.
